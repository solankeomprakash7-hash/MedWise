HOW TO APPROACH AN ECG WITHOUT PANICKING · PART 4
WHAT IT TAKES TO CALL AN ECG NORMAL · about 90 seconds (80 after edit)
/ = breath     [pause] = one full second     CAPS = lean on the word


Your senior asks, "ECG normal?"
You glance at it. / Nothing jumps out.
So you say yes.
[pause]
Could you DEFEND that?

Don't panic.
To call an ECG normal, /
it has to pass nine checks.
Fail one, /
and it isn't normal yet.


ONE. Rate, between 60 and 100.

TWO. A P wave before every QRS, /
upright in lead II.
That's sinus.

THREE. Lead I positive, / aVF positive.
Normal axis.
If aVF is negative, /
check lead II before you panic.
Still positive? Still normal.

FOUR. PR, / three to five small boxes.

FIVE. QRS, / under three. / Narrow.

SIX. R waves grow from V1 to V5, /
and cross over around V3, V4.

SEVEN. ST segments sit flat on the baseline. /
In EVERY lead.

EIGHT. T waves upright /
in I, II, and V3 to V6.

NINE. QTc under 450 in men, /
460 in women.


Now, a few things that look wrong /
and are completely normal.
A T wave upside down in aVR.
It's ALWAYS like that.
Tiny q waves in V5 and V6.
That's just the septum.
A heart rate that speeds up and slows down /
as the patient breathes.
Sinus arrhythmia. / Normal.


All nine pass? /
Then say "normal ECG" /
and mean it.

And if there's an old ECG, /
put them side by side.
Something that's changed since last time /
matters more than any single number.
